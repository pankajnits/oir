Abstract (paste into arXiv form):
We ask whether multi-hop answering over knowledge graphs needs readable predicates, or only equality among identifiers. Opaque isomorphic reasoning (OIR) is a measurement protocol: a renaming of entities and relations that we verify to be injective on each instance's finite atom set, with no legend in the prompt. HMAC-SHA256 is one generator of that map, not a cryptographic or privacy primitive. Finding. Unique sealed routes still join. Competing sealed routes typically yield UNKNOWN, not the decoy. With the start already in q, OpenAI opaque unique is 32/32; both-opaque unique is 32/32 for Composer 2.5, OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol, and Grok 4.5. A city-typed OpenAI freeze at n=200 (every HQ is Wikidata P31 city-like; QIDs re-checked live) is unique English/opaque 200/200, English two-path 199/200, named-arm opaque two-path 38/200 (Wilson [0.14,0.25]; UNKNOWN 151, decoy 11), plan 200/200. The n=26 city slice of the n=32 lock had decoy 0; that sample cannot resolve a ~5% decoy rate. At n=200 the ratio is about 14:1 UNKNOWN to decoy. Exact-match under two sealed routes is strongly sensitive to prompt surface form. At n=200, same-date 512-token case-id wording (ARM line kept) is named-arm 38/200 vs. hashed-id H5-cyclic 134/200. At 4096/medium the ARM line is non-monotonic and interacts with decoy type (cyclic/constant: no ARM 174/151, H5 129/44, K2 179/144). English relation names restore OpenAI with anonymous entities (OE two-path 32/32 vs. both-opaque 1/32). We score the answer node, not an emitted (r1, r2). Seals remove lexical cues; they are not a confidentiality proof.
