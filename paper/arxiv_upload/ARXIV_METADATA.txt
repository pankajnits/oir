Abstract (paste into arXiv form):
We ask whether 2-hop answering over small labeled graphs needs readable relation names, or only equality among identifiers. Opaque isomorphic reasoning (OIR) replaces relation strings with keyed opaque tokens, withholds a legend, and scores the answer node rather than an emitted (r1, r2). Unique sealed routes still execute with the start in q (OpenAI unique English/opaque 200/200; hashed-id no-ARM 200/200 at 512 tokens; opaque unique 32/32; both-opaque unique 32/32 on three families). Under two competing sealed routes, exact-match is a prompt-packing result, not a packaging-free opacity law. A prompt that names the condition and uses readable case ids (named-arm) usually yields UNKNOWN, not the decoy: OpenAI 38/200 gold, UNKNOWN 151, decoy 11. The same graphs with hashed case ids (ARM line kept) are 134/200 (UNKNOWN 44, decoy 20). Entity names stay English and we score the answer node, not (r1, r2), so two-path exact-match cannot be attributed to a join over CONTEXT. Dropping the condition header, at 4096 tokens and reasoning effort medium, cyclic-decoy English two-path is 197/200 and opaque two-path is 174/200. English relation names restore OpenAI with anonymous entities (opaque-entity two-path 32/32 vs. both-opaque 1/32). HMAC-SHA256 is one generator of that renaming on each instance's finite atom set, not a cryptographic or privacy primitive. On the primary 2x2, seals hide relation strings; entity names remain. They are not a confidentiality proof.
