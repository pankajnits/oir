Abstract (paste into arXiv form):
We ask whether multi-hop answering over knowledge graphs needs readable names and predicates, or only equality among identifiers. We find that opacity blocks selection under path ambiguity, not unique-path traversal. Opaque isomorphic reasoning (OIR) is a measurement protocol: a renaming of entities and relations that we verify to be injective on each instance's finite atom set, with no legend in the prompt. HMAC-SHA256 is one generator of that map, not a cryptographic or privacy primitive.

With the start already in the query, unique-path traversal survives sealed relation strings (OpenAI English unique 31/32, opaque unique 32/32 on a Wikidata-derived CEO slice) and both-opaque unique paths are 32/32 for Cursor Composer 2.5, OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol, and Grok 4.5. When two same-type routes compete, relation-only opacity (entities remain English) yields OpenAI 6/32, mostly UNKNOWN (24/32) rather than decoy (2/32). That drop is OpenAI-shaped: Grok English two-path is already 4/32. OpenAI both-opaque two-path on the same graphs is 1/32. We score the answer node, not an emitted (r1,r2). Seals remove lexical cues; they are not a confidentiality proof.
