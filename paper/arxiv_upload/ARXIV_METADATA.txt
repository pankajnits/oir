Abstract (paste into arXiv form):
We ask whether 2-hop answering over small labeled graphs needs readable relation strings, or only equality among identifiers. Opaque isomorphic reasoning (OIR) is a measurement protocol: a renaming of entities and relations that we verify to be injective on each instance's finite atom set, with no legend in the prompt. HMAC-SHA256 is one generator of that map, not a cryptographic or privacy primitive. We score the answer node, not an emitted (r1, r2). Finding. Unique sealed routes still join (OpenAI unique English/opaque 200/200 on a city-typed n=200 freeze, hashed-id no-ARM 200/200 at 512 tokens; opaque unique 32/32 with the start in q; both-opaque unique 32/32 on three families). Under two competing sealed routes, named-arm OpenAI (readable case ids plus an ARM line that names two 2-hops; 512 tokens) is 38/200 (UNKNOWN 151, decoy 11). The same graphs with hashed ids, ARM line kept, 512 tokens, are 134/200 (UNKNOWN 44, decoy 20). With hashed ids and no ARM, at 4096/medium, cyclic-decoy English two-path is 197/200 and opaque two-path is 174/200. Unique no-ARM at 512 is not a matched-budget contrast with two-path no-ARM at 4096/medium. English relation names restore OpenAI with anonymous entities (opaque-entity two-path 32/32 vs. both-opaque 1/32). On the primary 2x2, seals hide relation strings; entity names remain. They are not a confidentiality proof.
