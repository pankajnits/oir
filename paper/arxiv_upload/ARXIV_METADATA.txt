Abstract (paste into arXiv form):
We ask whether multi-hop answering over knowledge graphs needs readable predicates, or only equality among identifiers. Opaque isomorphic reasoning (OIR) is a measurement protocol: a renaming of entities and relations that we verify to be injective on each instance's finite atom set, with no legend in the prompt. HMAC-SHA256 is one generator of that map, not a cryptographic or privacy primitive.

With the start entity already named in the question, unique-path traversal survives sealed relation strings (OpenAI opaque unique 32/32) and both-opaque unique paths are 32/32 for Composer 2.5, OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol, and Grok 4.5. When two same-type routes compete and the prompt supplies no semantic-to-symbol map, the typical response is UNKNOWN rather than the decoy city. That end-to-end rate is packaging-sensitive: named-arm Wiki-H5 (the ARM line and case id name the condition) is OpenAI 6/32 (UNKNOWN 24/32, decoy 2/32); arm-neutral ids and header wording move the same graphs across 10--30/32. On the 26 city-valued items the named-arm cell is 5/26. The matched 2x2 is OpenAI-shaped: Grok named-arm English two-path is already 4/32 (arm-neutral 24/32). OpenAI both-opaque two-path is 1/32. We score the answer node, not an emitted (r1, r2). Seals remove lexical cues; they are not a confidentiality proof.
