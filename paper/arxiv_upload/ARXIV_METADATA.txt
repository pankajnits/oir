Abstract (paste into arXiv form):
We ask whether multi-hop answering over knowledge graphs needs readable predicates, or only equality among identifiers. Opaque isomorphic reasoning (OIR) is a measurement protocol: a renaming of entities and relations that we verify to be injective on each instance's finite atom set, with no legend in the prompt. HMAC-SHA256 is one generator of that map, not a cryptographic or privacy primitive.

With the start entity already named in the question, unique-path traversal survives sealed relation strings (OpenAI opaque unique 32/32) and both-opaque unique paths are 32/32 for Composer 2.5, OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol, and Grok 4.5. When two same-type routes compete and the prompt supplies no semantic-to-symbol map, named-arm Wiki-H5 typically yields UNKNOWN rather than the decoy (OpenAI 6/32, UNKNOWN 24/32, decoy 2/32). At the same 512-token budget, OpenAI arm-neutral H5-cyclic ids are 20/32; the OpenAI 10--30/32 band is header×decoy at 4096/medium. Exact-match under two routes is family- and packaging-dependent, not a ranking: named-arm English two-path is OpenAI 31/32, Composer 26/32, Grok 4/32 (arm-neutral Grok 24/32). OpenAI both-opaque two-path is 1/32. We score the answer node, not an emitted (r1, r2). Seals remove lexical cues; they are not a confidentiality proof.
